# All Sources — National Scope Research (2026-09-02)

## Claim 1 — Closed-loop CHW → nurse → provider referral coordination nationally applicable

Overall verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (confidence: medium)

- Medicare Payment for Community Health Worker Services — CY2024 PFS Final Rule Summary (IMPaCT/CMS) — https://www.impactcarehq.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/CHI-PFS-Final-Rule-Summary_11.25-Update.pdf — Medicare Part B now pays for CHI services by CHWs under general supervision (CPT 98160–98162 / G0019, G0022, G0023, G0024, G0322); CHWs may be employed by a community-based organization.
- State Community Health Worker Policies: 2024–2025 Trends (NASHP) — https://nashp.org/state-community-health-worker-policies-2024-2025-policy-trends/ — CHW certification/payment rules vary state by state; no uniform national pathway.
- State Policies for Expanding Medicaid Coverage of CHW Services (KFF) — https://www.kff.org/medicaid/state-policies-for-expanding-medicaid-coverage-of-community-health-worker-chw-services/ — Medicaid CHW payment authorized in a subset of states, via differing mechanisms.
- Community Health Worker Training Program (HRSA) — https://bhw.hrsa.gov/programs/community-health-worker-training-program-chwtp — Federal funding for CHW training/outreach to underserved communities.
- Connecting Those at Risk to Care: Pathways Community HUB Quick Start Guide (AHRQ) — https://www.ahrq.gov/sites/default/files/wysiwyg/innovations/CommHub_QuickStart.pdf — AHRQ-documented pay-for-performance model where CHW "pathways" are closed only when risk factors are resolved.
- Pathways Community HUB (PCHI) — https://www.pchi-hub.org/ — Nationally operating CHW-based closed-loop care coordination model.
- IMPaCT Care RCT outcomes (in PFS summary) — https://www.impactcarehq.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/CHI-PFS-Final-Rule-Summary_11.25-Update.pdf — ~$2,500 saved/patient year 1, 66% of hospital days vs controls, 94% NPS across RCTs.
- A Human-Centered Approach for Designing a Social Care Referral System — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11753861/ — Closed-loop referral concept and platforms (Unite Us, NowPow, Pieces Iris, Reema Health).
- Unite Us — https://uniteus.com/ — National closed-loop referral network platform.
- Referral Leakage Benchmark Report 2026 (Linear Health) — https://linear.health/blog/referral-leakage-benchmark-report-2026 — Referral leakage remains a large, largely unsolved national problem.
- CMS Launches $50 Billion Rural Health Transformation Program — https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-launches-landmark-50-billion-rural-health-transformation-program — Sept 2025 launch; state strategies for rural care coordination.
- CMS Announces $50B in Awards to All 50 States — https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-announces-50-billion-awards-strengthen-rural-health-all-50-states — Awards from 2026, ~$200M average first-year, funding rural care-coordination innovation.
- Systematic review of community-to-facility neonatal referral completion — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4589085/ — CHW referral completion 34–97% (median 74%); US rural-specific data thinner.
- Care Coordination (AHRQ) — https://www.ahrq.gov/ncepcr/research/care-coordination/index.html — AHRQ framing of care coordination as core quality lever.
- Strengthening Access to CHW Services in NY Through Medicaid (CHCS) — https://www.chcs.org/strengthening-access-to-community-health-worker-services-in-new-york-through-medicaid/ — Medicare CHI (2024) plus growing state Medicaid pathways; coverage still state-specific.
## Claim 2 — Multimodal, native-language patient intake for US rural healthcare access

- Access to Care Among Adults with Limited English Proficiency — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9971409/ — National survey analysis found adults with LEP were much more likely to lack a usual source of care and miss preventive services; the study reports 25.6 million LEP individuals in the US.
- Healthcare Access in Rural Communities Overview - Rural Health Information Hub — https://www.ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/healthcare-access — Rural access guidance explicitly says rural residents need confidence communicating with providers, especially if they are not fluent in English or have limited health literacy.
- Health Literacy Research in Rural Areas — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8590389/ — Rural-focused literature notes lower literacy in rural adults and that plain-language plus telephone outreach can improve screening participation.
- Section 1557: Ensuring Meaningful Access for Individuals with Limited English Proficiency | HHS.gov — https://www.hhs.gov/civil-rights/for-individuals/section-1557/fs-limited-english-proficiency/index.html — HHS requires meaningful access for LEP patients, notices, and top-15-language taglines; it also discourages unqualified interpreters.
- CLAS Standards - Think Cultural Health — https://thinkculturalhealth.hhs.gov/clas/standards — HHS CLAS standards call for language assistance at no cost, understandable materials, and services responsive to digital, cultural, language, and health-literacy needs.
- Translation and Interpretation Services | Medicaid — https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/financial-management/medicaid-administrative-claiming/translation-and-interpretation-services — CMS/Medicaid says states may claim reimbursement for translation/interpretation, and CHIPRA enables higher federal match for certain language-services costs.
- TELEHEALTH FOR PROVIDERS: WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW — https://www.cms.gov/files/document/telehealth-toolkit-providers.pdf — CMS telehealth guidance explicitly discusses non-English-speaking patients, rural populations, and workflows that should support interpreter use.
- Overview of Health Coverage and Care for Individuals with Limited English Proficiency (LEP) | KFF — https://www.kff.org/racial-equity-and-health-policy/overview-of-health-coverage-and-care-for-individuals-with-limited-english-proficiency/ — KFF reports 25.7 million LEP people in 2021, with LEP people three times as likely to be uninsured.
- Enrollment and Access Barriers for People with Limited English Proficiency — https://www.macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Enrollment-and-Access-Barriers-for-People-with-Limited-English-Proficiency.pdf — MACPAC summarizes access barriers, Medicaid state variation, and 2024 Section 1557 requirements around top-language notices and accessible communications.
- A Systematic Review of the Impact of Patient-Physician Non-English Language Concordance on Quality of Care and Outcomes - PubMed — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31147980/ — Review found language-concordant care improved outcomes in most situations; 25 of 33 studies favored concordance on at least one outcome.
- Using Bi-Directional Text Messaging to Engage Patients | CareMessage — https://www.caremessage.org/using-bi-directional-text-messaging-to-engage-patients/ — Demonstrates a national multilingual texting platform already used by safety-net clinics and FQHC partners.
- Multilingual Scribe Sunoh.ai Drastically Reduces Physician Burnout — https://www.eclinicalworks.com/sunoh-ai-ai-scribe-used-in-multilingual-setting-drastically-reduces-physician-burnout/ — Vendor example of multilingual voice-based clinical workflow support, showing the market already has AI language tools even though full intake remains fragmented.

## Claim 3

- **Demographics of Mobile Device Ownership and Adoption in the United States | Pew Research Center**
  https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/
  Pew’s 2025 mobile fact sheet reports 91% of U.S. adults own a smartphone and 98% own a cellphone. It also shows a steep age gradient, with 78% of adults 65+ owning smartphones.

- **Americans’ Use of Mobile Technology and Home Broadband | Pew Research Center**
  https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/01/31/americans-use-of-mobile-technology-and-home-broadband/
  Pew reports 90% smartphone ownership and 80% home broadband in 2023, with rural adults at 73% home broadband and 15% of adults being smartphone-dependent. It is the clearest single source for community-type digital gaps.

- **Some digital divides between rural, urban and suburban America | Pew Research Center**
  https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/08/19/some-digital-divides-persist-between-rural-urban-and-suburban-america/
  Pew states rural adults remain less likely than suburban adults to have home broadband and less likely than urban adults to own a smartphone, tablet, or traditional computer. This supports the rural access gap even where nationwide adoption is high.

- **About 4 in 10 U.S. adults have a smartwatch | Pew Research Center**
  https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/about-4-in-10-u-s-adults-have-a-smartwatch/
  Pew’s 2026 chart says 37% of U.S. adults have a smartwatch. A companion chart shows 24% adoption among adults 65+, confirming that smartwatch BYOD is useful but not universal.

- **Just under half of adults under 50 have a smartwatch, while smaller shares of older adults say so | Pew Research Center**
  https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/just-under-half-of-adults-under-50-have-a-smartwatch-while-smaller-shares-of-older-adults-say-so/
  Pew breaks smartwatch ownership out by age: 44% for ages 18-29, 44% for ages 30-49, 35% for ages 50-64, and 24% for 65+. This underscores the older-adult adoption gap relevant to rural RPM.

- **Remote Patient Monitoring | CMS**
  https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coverage/telehealth/remote-patient-monitoring
  CMS says Medicare broadly covers RPM using connected medical devices that automatically transmit data, with separate setup, device-supply, and management components. CMS also says payment does not depend on the exact type of device used.

- **MLN901705 - Telehealth & Remote Monitoring | CMS**
  https://www.cms.gov/files/document/mln901705-telehealth-remote-monitoring.pdf
  CMS explains RPM/RTM rules: remote monitoring includes RPM and RTM, data must be electronically collected and uploaded securely, patient consent is required, and RPM cannot be billed concurrently with RTM for the same monitoring activity.

- **CMS Manual System**
  https://www.cms.gov/files/document/r11118cp.pdf
  CMS states the RTM code family includes CPT 98975, 98976, 98977, 98980, and 98981, and that these were designated as sometimes-therapy codes. This confirms RTM is an established national Medicare code path alongside RPM.

- **MEDICARE TELEMEDICINE HEALTH CARE PROVIDER FACT SHEET | CMS**
  https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-telemedicine-health-care-provider-fact-sheet
  CMS defines HCPCS G2010 as remote evaluation of recorded video and/or images submitted by an established patient, explicitly described as store-and-forward, with a follow-up requirement. The fact sheet also reflects that standard Medicare telehealth is otherwise interactive and synchronous.

- **Asynchronous direct-to-consumer telehealth | Telehealth.HHS.gov**
  https://telehealth.hhs.gov/providers/best-practice-guides/direct-to-consumer/asynchronous-direct-to-consumer-telehealth
  HHS says asynchronous telehealth is also known as store-and-forward and is often used for intake or follow-up. It specifically recognizes text, image, and report upload workflows as legitimate telehealth patterns.

- **What are different types of telehealth? | Telehealth.HHS.gov**
  https://telehealth.hhs.gov/patients/what-are-different-types-telehealth
  HHS lists remote patient monitoring as a standard telehealth type and describes RPM as using tools to collect and share health data from home. This is helpful for framing BYOD RPM as mainstream telehealth rather than a niche workaround.

- **FCC Internet Access Services: Status as of June 30, 2024 | Federal Communications Commission**
  https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-411463A1.pdf
  FCC’s June 2024 internet-access report is the official national data source for broadband availability and connection speeds. It provides the underlying federal baseline for evaluating rural connectivity constraints.

- **FCC Releases Adopted 2024 Section 706 Report | TLP Law**
  https://www.tlp.law/2024/03/27/fcc-releases-adopted-2024-section-706-report/
  This summary of the FCC’s 2024 report quotes rural access figures of 83.1% at 25/3 Mbps and 72% at 100/20 Mbps. I used it to capture the rural-specific figures in readable form.

- **Tenovi Gateway - Cellular Connectivity for 60+ RPM Devices | Tenovi**
  https://www.tenovi.com/device/tenovi-gateway/
  Tenovi says its cellular gateway connects to 60+ FDA-cleared RPM and RTM devices, requires no app or Wi-Fi, and sends data over cellular networks. This is a strong example of a national BYOD-lite / low-friction RPM kit model.

- **Remote Healthcare - Optimize Health | Optimize Health**
  https://optimize.health/remote-healthcare/
  Optimize Health markets remote patient monitoring software plus patient-friendly devices and support. It supports the broader claim that nationally distributed RPM vendors already package software, device logistics, and support together.

- **Benefits of Remote Patient Monitoring | Healthie**
  https://www.gethealthie.com/glossary/remote-monitoring
  Healthie says remote monitoring can use wearable devices, mobile apps, and home-based monitoring devices, and that data are transmitted to clinicians for decision-making. This is evidence of a platform layer that can support BYOD workflows.
